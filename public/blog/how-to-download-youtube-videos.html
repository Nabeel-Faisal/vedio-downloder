Why people download YouTube videos

Before we get to the steps, it helps to understand why downloading is so popular — because the use case shapes the right method. The most common reasons in 2026:

  • Offline viewing on flights, commutes, or in areas with poor signal.
  • Editing — content creators grabbing clean clips for video edits, reaction videos or compilations.
  • Archiving — saving lectures, tutorials and important content before it gets taken down.
  • Audio extraction — converting videos into MP3 files for music, podcasts and audiobooks.

YouTube Premium offers official downloads, but only inside the YouTube app and only as long as your subscription is active. For real, permanent files you can save anywhere, you need a different approach.

What quality should you pick?

This depends on what you're doing with the video. Here's a quick cheat sheet:

  • 1080p HD (MP4) — the sweet spot for most people. Sharp on any screen up to 32", reasonable file size.
  • 4K Ultra HD — use only if the original was filmed in 4K and you have a 4K display. File sizes are 4–10x larger.
  • 720p — good for older phones or limited storage. Looks fine on small screens.
  • 360p / 240p — only if you have very limited bandwidth or storage. Quality is noticeably soft.
  • MP3 (audio) — perfect for music, podcasts, lectures or anything where you don't need video.

If you're unsure, Best Quality (auto) picks the highest available resolution automatically.

Can you download YouTube videos on iPhone or Android?

Yes, and it works exactly the same way. Open Safari (iPhone) or Chrome (Android), go to the downloader, paste the YouTube link, choose your quality, and tap Download. The video saves to your Files app on iPhone or the Downloads folder on Android. From there you can move it to Photos, share it, or play it offline.

One small tip for iPhone: when the file finishes downloading, tap the download icon in Safari's address bar, then Save to Files. iOS doesn't auto-save downloads to the Photos app, but you can move them there afterward.

Common problems and how to fix them

"Sign in to confirm you're not a bot"

This is YouTube's anti-bot check, which can sometimes affect downloaders. The fix is usually just to try again a moment later — the tool retries with different settings automatically. If a specific video keeps failing, try a different quality.

Download starts but the file is much smaller than expected

You probably picked an audio-only or video-only format. Always pick the option labeled with a clear quality (e.g., 1080p) or the Best Quality (auto) option for a complete file with sound.

The video plays without sound after downloading

Older browsers sometimes can't play modern codecs. Try opening the file in VLC Media Player — it plays everything. If you want a more compatible file, re-download at 720p (which uses the broadly-compatible H.264 codec with AAC audio).

Is it legal to download YouTube videos?

The short answer: it depends on what you're downloading and what you do with it.

You can legally download:

  • Your own videos (obviously)
  • Videos with a Creative Commons license
  • Content in the public domain
  • Videos for personal use under fair use / fair dealing (jurisdiction-dependent)

What's not okay: downloading copyrighted material and redistributing it, monetizing it, or claiming it as your own. Always respect copyright and the source platform's terms of service. This guide is for personal, fair-use downloads only.

Why not just use a browser extension?

Browser extensions were popular a few years ago, but most major browsers (Chrome, Edge, Brave, Firefox) have either removed or restricted video-downloader extensions due to YouTube's policies. Even when they exist, they often:

  • Stop working when YouTube changes its player code (which happens often)
  • Request invasive permissions (read everything on every page)
  • Get pulled from extension stores without warning
  • Are bundled with adware or tracking

A web-based tool runs on the server side, gets updated centrally, and never touches your browser permissions. It's safer and more reliable.

Final tips

  • Stick to MP4 for maximum device compatibility.
  • For music-only, MP3 saves a lot of storage (~10x smaller than video).
  • If a download fails, try a different quality — sometimes specific formats are temporarily unavailable.
